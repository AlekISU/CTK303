What is Reflective Design?

Reflective design, as described by Don Norman, operates at the level of meaning and memory by engaging users in deeper thought and identity, personal history, and emotional connection. In this case when combined with nostalgia, reflective design becomes a powerful tool for storytelling and self-recognition.

How can this apply to Web Design?

Nostalgia in web design often draws on visual and interactive cues from earlier digital eras such as pixelated graphics, retro color palettes, lo-fi sound effects, or layouts inspired by early 2000s personal websites. However, nostalgic design goes beyond aesthetic imitation. It intentionally prompts users to remember who they were at a particular moment in time. For example, a website might recreate the feeling of scrolling through your favorite childhood forums, using familiar interface elements that echo early social media platforms or old operating systems. These elements don't just imitate the past; they invite users to reflect on their growth, relationships, and shifting identities.

Reflective web design can harness this by pairing familiar visuals with interactive storytelling—perhaps asking users to revisit old memories, contribute their own stories, or explore archived digital artifacts. Through this process, the website becomes more than a functional interface; it becomes a space for personal reflection.

Why this Website?

As a child, my favorite pastime was playing online multiplayer games like Build-a-Bearville, Toon Town, Wizard 101, and any other free game I could get my greedy little hands on. Any time I had free time (which was often), I would either login to one of those games or spend hours searching the internet for the next game I could add to my archive. One of the things that helped me decide which games I wanted to play was the design of the website. The bright colors and funky text made me curious about what the experience could be. Was the game as fun as the text choice? As cute as the color palette? As chaotic as the layout? Those are the questions I would ask myself and it piqued my interest.

It reminds me of a simpler time when I had no worries and all the free time in the world. I wasn’t aware of how scary the world could be and my naivety made me see everything in a bright, colorful, and positive light. Reflecting on those times helps me see how much I’ve grown and appreciate those experiences I had that molded the person I am today.
